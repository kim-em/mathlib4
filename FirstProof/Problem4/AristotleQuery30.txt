FINITE FREE STAM INEQUALITY — Residue formula + induction on degree

=== GOAL ===

Prove the single sorry: finite_free_stam_inequality for all n ≥ 2.
  invPhi (boxplus n p q) ≥ invPhi p + invPhi q

=== KEY NEW TOOL: Residue formula for Phi ===

The context now contains Phi_eq_sum_roots_deriv:

  Phi(p) = -1/4 * Σ_{μ ∈ roots(p')} p''(μ) / p(μ)

where the sum is over the n-1 critical points μ of p (roots of p').

This relates Phi of a degree-n poly to evaluations at roots of p' (degree n-1).

=== STRATEGY: Induction via derivative ===

STEP 1: For the derivative p', define a modified "Phi-like" quantity.
  p' has degree n-1 with roots μ_1,...,μ_{n-1} (critical points of p).
  These interlace the roots of p (by Rolle's theorem, since p splits over ℝ).

STEP 2: From the residue formula:
  Phi(p) = -1/4 * Σ_j p''(μ_j) / p(μ_j)

  Note: p''(μ_j) / p(μ_j) involves evaluating p at critical points.
  If we could relate this to Phi(p'), we'd have a recursive formula.

  Actually, p''(x)/p(x) at a root μ of p' can be written as:
    p''(μ)/p(μ) = [p''(μ)/p'(μ)] / [p(μ)/p'(μ)]  ... but p'(μ) = 0!

  Wait, the formula says Σ over roots of p'. At μ_j with p'(μ_j) = 0:
    The residue of p''(x)²/(p(x)·p'(x)) at x = μ_j is p''(μ_j)²/(p(μ_j)·p''(μ_j)) = p''(μ_j)/p(μ_j)
    (assuming μ_j is a simple root of p', which holds when p has distinct roots).

  So Phi(p) = -1/4 * Σ_j p''(μ_j)/p(μ_j).

STEP 3: Now consider boxplus. We need to show:
  1/Phi(p⊞q) ≥ 1/Phi(p) + 1/Phi(q)

  Using the residue formula:
  Phi(p⊞q) = -1/4 * Σ_k (p⊞q)''(ρ_k) / (p⊞q)(ρ_k)
  where ρ_k are roots of (p⊞q)'.

STEP 4: Key question: How do the critical points of p⊞q relate to those of p and q?
  (p⊞q)' = derivative of boxplus. Is there a formula for (p⊞q)' in terms of p' and q'?

  From the coefficient definition of boxplus:
    (p⊞q)(x) = Σ_k C(n,k) * scaled_coeff(p,k) * scaled_coeff(q,n-k) * x^k ... no, it's more complex.

  Actually, derivatives and boxplus don't commute simply. But:
  - Cumulants of p⊞q are sums: κ_k(p⊞q) = κ_k(p) + κ_k(q)
  - Cumulants of p' relate to cumulants of p via: κ_k(p') = (n·κ_k(p))/(n-k) ... or similar.

STEP 5: If we can express Phi in terms of cumulants using the residue formula,
  and if the resulting expression is superadditive in cumulants, we're done.

  From the residue formula, Phi depends on:
  (a) The critical points μ_j (roots of p')
  (b) The values p(μ_j) and p''(μ_j)

  Both (a) and (b) are determined by the coefficients, hence by the cumulants.
