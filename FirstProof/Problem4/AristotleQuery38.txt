FINITE FREE STAM INEQUALITY — General n via electrostatic/log-concavity

=== GOAL ===

Prove the single sorry: finite_free_stam_inequality for all n ≥ 2.
  invPhi (boxplus n p q) ≥ invPhi p + invPhi q

=== STRATEGY: Log-concavity of discriminant under free convolution ===

For a monic polynomial p with distinct real roots λ₁ < ... < λₙ:
  Φ(p) = Σᵢ (p''(λᵢ)/(2p'(λᵢ)))² = Σᵢ (Σⱼ≠ᵢ 1/(λᵢ-λⱼ))²

The discriminant is: Δ(p) = Π_{i<j} (λᵢ-λⱼ)²
And: log Δ(p) = Σ_{i<j} 2·log|λᵢ-λⱼ|

ELECTROSTATIC INTERPRETATION:
The roots behave like charged particles on a line.
The "energy" is E(p) = -log Δ(p) = -Σ_{i<j} log(λᵢ-λⱼ)²
The "force" on particle i is: Fᵢ = Σⱼ≠ᵢ 2/(λᵢ-λⱼ)
And: Φ(p) = (1/4) Σᵢ Fᵢ²

So: Φ(p) = (1/4) ||∇E||² (squared gradient of energy)

THE STAM INEQUALITY says: 1/||∇E(p⊞q)||² ≥ 1/||∇E(p)||² + 1/||∇E(q)||²

This is a CAUCHY-SCHWARZ TYPE inequality for gradient norms under addition.

In the classical information-theoretic setting:
  1/I(X+Y) ≥ 1/I(X) + 1/I(Y)  where I = Fisher information
This follows from the Cramér-Rao bound or from convexity of Fisher information.

ANALOGY: The free convolution p⊞q is the "free" analogue of X+Y.
The functional Φ is the "free Fisher information."
The Stam inequality should follow from an analogous convexity/information inequality.

=== APPROACH: Direct Cauchy-Schwarz on score functions ===

Define the score function: s_p(x) = p'(x)/p(x) = Σᵢ 1/(x-λᵢ)
Then: s_p'(x) = -Σᵢ 1/(x-λᵢ)² = -p''(x)/p(x) + (p'(x)/p(x))²

At roots λᵢ: s_p(λᵢ) is undefined but p'(λᵢ)/p(λᵢ) makes sense in the limit.
Actually: lim_{x→λᵢ} (x-λᵢ)·s_p(x) = 1, so s_p has simple poles at roots.

FROM THE RESIDUE FORMULA (Phi_eq_sum_roots_deriv):
  Φ(p) = -1/4 · Σ_μ p''(μ)/p(μ)  where μ ranges over critical points of p.

IDEA: Relate the critical point values of p⊞q to those of p and q using
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality applied to the root structure.

For boxplus with cumulant additivity:
  κ_k(p⊞q) = κ_k(p) + κ_k(q)

The coefficients of p⊞q are polynomial functions of the κ_k(p)+κ_k(q).
The critical points and their values are algebraic functions of the coefficients.
