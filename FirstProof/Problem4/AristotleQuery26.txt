FINITE FREE STAM INEQUALITY — Induction on n via derivative/critical points

=== GOAL ===

Prove the single sorry: finite_free_stam_inequality for all n ≥ 2.
  invPhi (boxplus n p q) ≥ invPhi p + invPhi q

=== STRATEGY: Relate Phi_n to Phi_{n-1} via derivatives ===

KEY INSIGHT: If p has degree n with roots λ_1,...,λ_n, then p' has degree n-1
with roots μ_1,...,μ_{n-1} that INTERLACE the λ_i (by Rolle's theorem).

Define the "root-score" S_i = Σ_{j≠i} 1/(λ_i - λ_j) = p'(λ_i)/p(... wait, no.
Actually S_i = (d/dx log p)|'s residue contribution.

More precisely: p'(x)/p(x) = Σ_i 1/(x - λ_i), so p'(λ_i)/lim = Σ_{j≠i} 1/(λ_i - λ_j) = S_i.

And Phi(p) = Σ S_i^2.

Similarly for p': p''(x)/p'(x) = Σ_j 1/(x - μ_j) where μ_j are roots of p'.

Actually from Phi_derivative_form: Phi = Σ (p''(λ_i)/(2p'(λ_i)))^2.

And p''(x) = Σ_{j} p'(x)/(x-λ_j) - p(x)·stuff, evaluated at x = λ_i gives:
  p''(λ_i) = 2 Σ_{j≠i} p'(λ_i)/(λ_i - λ_j) · (1/(λ_i-λ_j)) ...

No, let me use partial fractions directly:
  p'(x) = Σ_i ∏_{j≠i} (x - λ_j)

At x = λ_i:
  p'(λ_i) = ∏_{j≠i} (λ_i - λ_j)
  p''(λ_i) = 2 Σ_{k≠i} ∏_{j≠i,j≠k} (λ_i - λ_j) = 2 p'(λ_i) · Σ_{k≠i} 1/(λ_i - λ_k)

So p''(λ_i)/(2p'(λ_i)) = Σ_{k≠i} 1/(λ_i - λ_k) = S_i.

Great! So Phi(p) = Σ_i S_i^2 where S_i = Σ_{j≠i} 1/(λ_i - λ_j).

INDUCTION IDEA:
The derivative p' has roots μ_1,...,μ_{n-1} interlacing λ_1,...,λ_n.
Define Phi_{n-1}(p') = Σ_j T_j^2 where T_j = Σ_{k≠j} 1/(μ_j - μ_k).

QUESTION: Is there an identity relating Σ S_i^2 to Σ T_j^2 + correction?

If Phi_n(p) = Phi_{n-1}(p') + correction(p), and the correction is well-behaved
under boxplus, we could use induction.

KEY FACT ABOUT BOXPLUS AND DERIVATIVES:
If p⊞q denotes the finite free convolution, what is (p⊞q)'?
Since boxplus is defined on coefficients, (p⊞q)' is the formal derivative.
But is there a nice relation to p' and q'?

Actually: for the CONTINUOUS free convolution, the R-transform satisfies
R_{μ⊞ν} = R_μ + R_ν. The Cauchy transform G satisfies 1/G = z - R(G).
The derivative corresponds to "removing a root" or "differentiation of the Cauchy transform."

For FINITE free convolution: The finite free cumulants κ_k satisfy
κ_k(p⊞q) = κ_k(p) + κ_k(q). The relationship between cumulants of p and p'
is via the "finite free derivative" or the binomial formula.

=== LEAN APPROACH ===

Step 1: Prove that S_i = p''(λ_i)/(2p'(λ_i)) = Σ_{j≠i} 1/(λ_i - λ_j).
  (Should follow from Phi_derivative_form and the computation above.)

Step 2: Try to express Phi(p) in terms of the roots of p' and the roots of p.
  Use interlacing: between consecutive λ_i, λ_{i+1} lies exactly one μ_j.

Step 3: Establish any recurrence Phi_n = f(Phi_{n-1}, extra_data).

Step 4: Show the recurrence preserves the invPhi superadditivity.

ALTERNATIVE: Instead of pure induction, use the identity
  Σ_i S_i = Σ_i Σ_{j≠i} 1/(λ_i - λ_j) = 0  (by antisymmetry)

and try Cauchy-Schwarz:
  0 = (Σ S_i)^2 ≤ n · Σ S_i^2 = n · Phi(p)

This just gives Phi ≥ 0, not useful.

But a WEIGHTED Cauchy-Schwarz with carefully chosen weights might give the
inequality we need. The weights should come from comparing p⊞q roots to p roots.
