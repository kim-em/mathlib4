FINITE FREE STAM INEQUALITY — General n Case

Problem 4 from "First Proof" (arXiv:2602.05192):
For monic real-rooted degree-n polynomials p, q with distinct roots,
prove that 1/Φ_n(p ⊞_n q) ≥ 1/Φ_n(p) + 1/Φ_n(q).

This is the finite polynomial analog of the free Stam inequality
(Mingo-Speicher, Theorem 19 / equation 8.19).

PRIORITY: Prove the GENERAL n case. Small cases (n=1,2,3) are already handled
in the attached context file. We need a proof for ALL n.

=== DEFINITIONS ===

Φ_n(p) = Σ_i (Σ_{j≠i} 1/(λ_i - λ_j))²  for roots λ_1,...,λ_n of p

(p ⊞_n q)(x) = Σ_{k=0}^n c_k x^{n-k} where
  c_k = Σ_{i+j=k} ((n-i)!(n-j)!)/(n!(n-k)!) · a_i · b_j

=== KEY IDENTITIES ===

1. Φ_n = 2 Σ_{i<j} 1/(λ_i - λ_j)²
   (Cross terms cancel in the double sum.)

2. Derivative form: Φ_n(p) = Σ_i [p''(λ_i)/(2p'(λ_i))]²
   where p'(λ_i) = Π_{j≠i}(λ_i - λ_j).

3. Gradient of log-Vandermonde:
   ξ_i := Σ_{j≠i} 1/(λ_i - λ_j) = (1/2) ∂/∂λ_i log Δ(λ)
   where Δ(λ) = Π_{i<j} (λ_i - λ_j)² is the squared Vandermonde.
   Then Φ_n = |ξ|² = Σ_i ξ_i².

4. Random matrix representation (Marcus-Spielman-Srivastava, Thm 1.2):
   p ⊞_n q = E_Q[det(xI - A - QBQ*)]
   where A = diag(λ_1,...,λ_n), B = diag(μ_1,...,μ_n), Q uniform over O(n).

5. U-transform (Marcus, Lemma 3.4):
   [p ⊞_n q](x) = E{(x - S - T)^n}
   where S, T are INDEPENDENT random variables.

6. Finite free R-transform is ADDITIVE under ⊞_n:
   R_{p⊞q}^(n) = R_p^(n) + R_q^(n)

7. Hermite equality case:
   a^n H_n(x/a) ⊞_n b^n H_n(x/b) = c^n H_n(x/c) where c = √(a²+b²).

=== PROOF STRATEGIES (ordered by promise) ===

STRATEGY 1 — Finite Conjugate Variables (mirrors the infinite proof):

The infinite free Stam inequality (Mingo-Speicher Thm 19) is proved using
conjugate systems. The key idea transfers to finite setting:

Define the "score" at root λ_i:
  ξ_i = Σ_{j≠i} 1/(λ_i - λ_j)

For the random matrix representation A + QBQ* with Q uniform over O(n):
- The eigenvalues ν_1,...,ν_n of A + QBQ* have their own scores ξ_i^ν
- By a conditional expectation argument:
  ξ_i^ν ≈ E[ξ_i^λ | eigenvalues ν]

The conditional variance inequality gives:
  Σ_i (ξ_i^ν)² ≤ Σ_i (ξ_i^λ)²
i.e., Φ_n(ν) ≤ Φ_n(λ) for each realization of Q.

Taking expectations and using Jensen's inequality on 1/Φ (which is concave
if Φ > 0):
  1/Φ_n(p ⊞_n q) = 1/Φ_n(E_Q[...]) ≥ E_Q[1/Φ_n(ν)]

Then we need to show E_Q[1/Φ_n(eigenvalues of A+QBQ*)] ≥ 1/Φ_n(A) + 1/Φ_n(B).

This last step might use the independence structure: for generic Q,
the eigenvalue scores of A+QBQ* decompose into contributions from A and B.

STRATEGY 2 — Finite Free Cumulants / R-Transform:

The R-transform is additive: κ_k(p ⊞_n q) = κ_k(p) + κ_k(q).

Step 1: Express 1/Φ_n as a function f(κ_1,...,κ_n) of finite free cumulants.
Step 2: Show f is superadditive: f(κ+κ') ≥ f(κ) + f(κ').

For n=2: κ_1 = -e_1, κ_2 = e_1² - 2e_2. Then 1/Φ₂ = κ₂/2 is LINEAR in
cumulants, giving equality.

For general n: if 1/Φ_n is a concave function of the cumulant vector,
superadditivity follows. The key would be computing the Hessian of 1/Φ_n
in cumulant coordinates and showing it's negative semidefinite.

STRATEGY 3 — Electrostatic / Schur-convexity:

Φ_n = 2 Σ_{i<j} 1/(λ_i-λ_j)² is the electrostatic energy of unit charges.

Claim: 1/Φ_n is Schur-concave as a function of the root tuple.
If true, then since ⊞_n "spreads" roots (by the interlacing results of MSS),
the root tuple of p ⊞_n q majorizes neither p's nor q's roots individually,
but the spreading effect gives the inequality.

More concretely: define E(λ) = Σ_{i<j} (λ_i-λ_j)^{-2}.
Show that 1/E is Schur-concave, i.e., the Schur derivative is ≤ 0:
  (∂/∂λ_i - ∂/∂λ_j)(1/E) · (λ_i - λ_j) ≤ 0 for all i,j.

STRATEGY 4 — Direct computation via power sums:

Express Φ_n in terms of the power sums p_k = Σ_i λ_i^k and use
Newton's identities to relate to elementary symmetric polynomials e_k.

Under ⊞_n, the e_k transform via the convolution formula.
The power sums p_k of p ⊞_n q can be expressed in terms of p_k(λ) and p_k(μ).

For reduced (zero-trace) polynomials, p_1 = 0 and:
  Φ_n = 2 Σ_{i<j} (λ_i-λ_j)^{-2}

Use the identity: Σ_{i<j} (λ_i-λ_j)^{-2} = [Σ_i ξ_i² - (Σ_i ξ_i)²/n] + ...
where the correction involves higher power sums of the gaps.

STRATEGY 5 — Information-theoretic / Cramér-Rao:

Classical Stam inequality: 1/J(X+Y) ≥ 1/J(X) + 1/J(Y) where J = Fisher info.
Proof: Cramér-Rao bound J(X) ≥ 1/Var(X), plus Var(X+Y) = Var(X) + Var(Y).

Finite analog:
- Φ_n plays the role of Fisher information
- Need a "finite Cramér-Rao": Φ_n ≥ 1/V_n for some variance-like V_n
- Need V_n to be additive under ⊞_n
- Then: 1/Φ_n ≤ V_n, and V_n(p⊞q) = V_n(p) + V_n(q)

The candidate for V_n: related to the variance of the empirical root distribution
μ_p = (1/n)Σ δ_{λ_i}. The second moment m_2 = (1/n)Σ λ_i² is related to
the sub-leading coefficient, which IS additive under ⊞_n for reduced polynomials.

But this only gives an upper bound 1/Φ_n ≤ V_n, and we need a LOWER bound
1/Φ_n(p⊞q) ≥ .... So this approach needs refinement — perhaps a tighter
Cramér-Rao analog specific to the finite free setting.

=== NOTES ===

- This is believed to be an OPEN problem. A complete proof would be a significant result.
- The n=2 case is an equality; for n≥3 strict inequality holds generically.
- The Hermite polynomial case gives equality for all n (analog of Gaussian+Gaussian).
