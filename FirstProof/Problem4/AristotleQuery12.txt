FINITE FREE STAM INEQUALITY — General n proof

=== THE PROBLEM ===

For monic real-rooted degree-n polynomials p, q with distinct roots, prove:
  1/Phi_n(p ⊞_n q) >= 1/Phi_n(p) + 1/Phi_n(q)

where Phi_n(p) = sum_i (sum_{j≠i} 1/(λ_i - λ_j))^2 and ⊞_n is the finite free
additive convolution defined by:
  (p ⊞_n q)(x) = sum_{k=0}^{n} C(n,k) * (sum_{i+j=k} C(n-i,n-k)*C(n-j,n-k)/C(n,n-k)^2 * a_i * b_j) * x^{n-k}

=== PROOF APPROACHES ===

APPROACH 1 — Random matrix / expectation (MOST PROMISING for general n):

The finite free convolution has a random matrix representation:
  (p ⊞_n q)(x) = E_Q[det(xI - A - Q*B*Q^T)]
where A, B are diagonal matrices of roots and Q is Haar-distributed orthogonal.

The key identity: Phi_n(p) = trace((p''(Λ)/(2p'(Λ)))^2) where Λ = diag(roots).

For the convolution: the roots of p ⊞_n q are expectations of eigenvalues of A + QBQ^T.
By Jensen's inequality or variance decomposition:
  Phi_n(p ⊞_n q) relates to conditional expectations of Phi applied to A + QBQ^T.

The Stam inequality in information theory is proved via the identity:
  1/Fisher(X+Y) = E[1/Fisher(X|X+Y)] (conditional Fisher info)
and Jensen: E[1/Fisher(X|X+Y)] >= 1/E[Fisher(X|X+Y)] = 1/Fisher(X).

Try the ANALOGOUS approach: define a conditional Phi for the random matrix model
and use Jensen's inequality for the convex function 1/x.

APPROACH 2 — Heat flow / Dyson Brownian motion:

For the infinite free case, the Stam inequality follows from the free heat flow.
The finite analog: roots of p_t = p ⊞_n (X^n + t*X^{n-2}) satisfy a system of ODEs
(finite Dyson Brownian motion). Under this flow:
  d/dt (1/Phi_n(p_t)) = something nonneg
which gives superadditivity of 1/Phi_n.

The derivative computation uses Phi_derivative_form and the ODE for roots.

APPROACH 3 — Cauchy-Schwarz on the pairwise form:

Phi_n(p) = sum_i (sum_{j≠i} 1/(λ_i-λ_j))^2. Define:
  S_i(p) = sum_{j≠i} 1/(λ_i - λ_j) = p''(λ_i)/(2p'(λ_i))

Then 1/Phi = 1/(sum_i S_i^2). For p ⊞_n q with roots μ_k:
  S_i(p ⊞_n q) relates to S(p) and S(q) via the convolution.

By Cauchy-Schwarz: (sum a_i^2)(sum b_i^2) >= (sum a_i*b_i)^2.
This gives inequalities between Phi values.

The Titu's lemma approach:
  sum_i 1/S_i^2 >= n^2 / (sum_i S_i^2) = n^2/Phi
and 1/Phi = (sum S_i^2)^{-1}. Not directly useful, but the structure
of S_i as p''/(2p') under convolution may yield the inequality.

APPROACH 4 — Cumulant coordinates (extends proven n=3,4 cases):

In finite free cumulant coordinates (κ_2, ..., κ_n), the convolution is:
  κ_k(p ⊞_n q) = κ_k(p) + κ_k(q) for all k ≥ 2.
This is essentially proven (boxplus_centered_coeff_add for k=2, boxplus_centered_coeff_add_3 for k=3,
boxplus_centered_deg4_kappa4_additive for k=4).

Express 1/Phi_n as a function f(κ_2, ..., κ_n) of cumulants, then show
f is superadditive. For n=3: f(κ_2, κ_3) = (2/9)κ_2 - (3/2)κ_3^2/κ_2^2 (proven).
For n=4 symmetric: f(κ_2, κ_4) proven superadditive.

The pattern: 1/Phi = linear in κ_2 + nonpositive remainder, and the remainder
involves a convex ratio that satisfies Cauchy-Schwarz.

APPROACH 5 — Induction on n:

Base cases n=2 (equality), n=3 (proven). For the inductive step:
degree-n polynomials relate to degree-(n-1) via differentiation or root interlacing.
The Gauss-Lucas theorem and Rolle's theorem connect roots of p' to roots of p.

If Phi_{n-1}(p') relates nicely to Phi_n(p), induction may work.
