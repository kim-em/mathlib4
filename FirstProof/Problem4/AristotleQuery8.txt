FINITE FREE STAM INEQUALITY — General n Case via Abstract Methods

This is a continuation of previous Aristotle queries on the Finite Free Stam Inequality
(Problem 4 from arXiv:2602.05192, the "First Proof" benchmark).

The main theorem to prove: for monic real-rooted degree-n polynomials p, q with distinct roots,
  1/Phi_n(p ⊞_n q) >= 1/Phi_n(p) + 1/Phi_n(q)
where Phi_n(p) = sum_i (sum_{j!=i} 1/(lambda_i - lambda_j))^2
and ⊞_n is finite free additive convolution.

=== STRATEGIC DIRECTION ===

We have already proven the inequality case-by-case for n=1,2,3 and n=4 symmetric quartics.
These proofs used explicit Phi formulas and Cauchy-Schwarz. That approach DOES NOT SCALE:
the algebraic complexity of Phi_n grows combinatorially with n. We need a fundamentally
different, ABSTRACT approach that works uniformly for all n.

DO NOT attempt more case-by-case algebraic computations.
DO focus on structural/abstract arguments that prove the result for ALL n simultaneously.

=== APPROACH 1: FINITE FREE FISHER INFORMATION AND DE BRUIJN'S IDENTITY ===

In classical information theory, the Stam inequality follows from de Bruijn's identity:
  d/dt h(X + sqrt(t) Z) = (1/2) J(X + sqrt(t) Z)
where h is entropy and J is Fisher information. The key insight:
  - Fisher information J is CONVEX (hence superadditive)
  - 1/J is CONCAVE (hence subadditive... wait, we need superadditive)
  - Actually: the entropy power inequality uses the fact that
    N(X) = (1/(2*pi*e)) * exp(2h(X)/n) is concave, not 1/J directly.

In the FREE probability setting (Mingo-Speicher, "Free Probability and Random Matrices"):
  - Free Fisher information Phi*(mu) = integral |H(mu)(x)|^2 d(mu)(x)
    where H(mu) is the Hilbert transform of mu
  - The free Stam inequality states: 1/Phi*(mu ⊞ nu) >= 1/Phi*(mu) + 1/Phi*(nu)
  - This follows from the FREE de Bruijn identity relating free entropy chi
    to free Fisher information

For the FINITE FREE case, define a discrete analog:
  - The "finite free Fisher information" IS Phi_n (it's the same object!)
  - Phi_n(p) = sum_i (H_p(lambda_i))^2 where H_p(x) = sum_{j!=i} 1/(x - lambda_j)
    is a discrete Hilbert transform
  - The finite free heat flow: p_t = p ⊞_n H_t where H_t is the scaled Hermite polynomial
    (already defined in our context as scaled_hermite)
  - Under this flow, the cumulants evolve as kappa_k(p_t) = kappa_k(p) + t * delta_{k,2}
    (only kappa_2 changes, by additivity of cumulants!)

STRATEGY: Prove a FINITE de Bruijn identity:
  d/dt [1/Phi_n(p_t)] |_{t=0} = [something non-negative related to cumulants]

If 1/Phi_n is concave along the heat flow (as a function of t), and the heat flow
adds independent "noise" (via kappa_2 additivity), then the Stam inequality follows
by the standard information-theoretic argument.

Key lemma to try:

theorem finite_deBruijn (n : N) (p : R[X]) (t : R) :
    let p_t := boxplus n p (scaled_hermite n t)
    d/dt [1 / Phi p_t] = [explicit formula in terms of roots of p_t]

=== APPROACH 2: RANDOM MATRIX REPRESENTATION ===

By Marcus-Spielman-Srivastava (arXiv:1504.00350, Theorem 1.2):
  p ⊞_n q = E_Q [det(xI - A - Q B Q*)]
where A, B are diagonal matrices with roots of p, q as entries, and Q is Haar-random
on the orthogonal group O(n).

This means:
  1/Phi_n(p ⊞_n q) = 1/Phi_n(E_Q[char poly of A + QBQ*])

The key idea: Phi_n is a CONVEX function of polynomial roots (it's a sum of squares
of rational functions). By Jensen's inequality applied to the random matrix expectation:

  Phi_n(E_Q[...]) <= E_Q[Phi_n(...)]

Wait — this gives the WRONG direction. We need 1/Phi to be superadditive, which means
Phi should be... Let's think more carefully.

Actually, the MSS representation tells us:
  (p ⊞_n q)(x) = E_Q[det(xI - (A + QBQ*))]

The roots of p ⊞_n q are NOT simply the eigenvalues of A + QBQ* for a single Q.
Rather, the POLYNOMIAL is the expectation. This is subtler.

NOTE: The Jensen argument using Phi convexity goes the WRONG direction for our purposes
(it would give an upper bound on Phi of the convolution, not a lower bound on 1/Phi).
The random matrix representation is more useful for INTUITION about why the inequality
should hold (randomized rotation "spreads out" eigenvalues, reducing electrostatic stress).

Keep this approach as background intuition, but focus formalization efforts elsewhere.

=== APPROACH 3: VARIATIONAL / ELECTROSTATIC ARGUMENT ===

Phi_n(p) has a physical interpretation: if the roots lambda_i are positions of
unit charges on a line, then Phi_n is related to the "electrostatic stress" —
the sum of squared forces on each charge.

The convolution ⊞_n is a "randomized addition" of charge configurations.
The Stam inequality says that randomized addition REDUCES electrostatic stress
(in the 1/Phi sense).

This is analogous to: adding independent noise reduces Fisher information (in the
reciprocal sense). The mechanism is "spreading out" the charges.

VARIATIONAL APPROACH:
- Define the "finite free entropy" chi_n(p) = ??? (analog of Voiculescu's free entropy)
- Prove chi_n is CONCAVE under ⊞_n (this would be the finite free entropy power inequality)
- Derive the Stam inequality as the infinitesimal version

The finite free entropy should satisfy:
  d/dt chi_n(p ⊞_n H_t) = (1/2) * (1/Phi_n(p ⊞_n H_t))

(finite de Bruijn identity — connects entropy to Fisher information along heat flow)

If chi_n(p ⊞_n H_t) is concave in t, then 1/Phi_n(p ⊞_n H_t) is decreasing in t,
which is a monotonicity property along the heat flow.

=== APPROACH 4: CUMULANT GENERATING FUNCTION ===

The proven cumulant additivity gives us a "Fourier-like" representation:
  p is determined by its cumulants (kappa_2, ..., kappa_n)
  p ⊞_n q has cumulants (kappa_2 + kappa_2', ..., kappa_n + kappa_n')

So the question reduces to: is 1/Phi_n, viewed as a function
  f : R^{n-1} -> R
  f(kappa_2, ..., kappa_n) = 1/Phi_n(p)
superadditive?

This is a question about a SINGLE real-valued function on R^{n-1}.

By homogeneity: Phi_n scales as Phi_n(c * roots) = Phi_n(roots)/c^2, so
  f(c^2 kappa_2, c^3 kappa_3, ..., c^n kappa_n) = c^2 * f(kappa_2, ..., kappa_n)

This means f is 2-HOMOGENEOUS with respect to the weighted scaling
  kappa_k -> c^k kappa_k.

A 2-homogeneous superadditive function satisfies f(x+y) >= f(x) + f(y).
By Euler's theorem for homogeneous functions:
  sum_k k * kappa_k * (partial f / partial kappa_k) = 2 * f

Also, superadditivity of a smooth function f is equivalent to:
  The Hessian D^2 f is positive semidefinite at all points
  (i.e., f is convex)
Wait — no! Superadditivity and convexity are DIFFERENT.
  - f convex: f(tx + (1-t)y) <= t f(x) + (1-t) f(y)
  - f superadditive: f(x+y) >= f(x) + f(y)

For 2-homogeneous functions: f(x+y) >= f(x) + f(y) is equivalent to
  f(x+y) - f(x) - f(y) >= 0
  = 2 * [symmetric bilinear form B(x,y)] where f(x) = B(x,x)
  (by polarization of the homogeneous form)

So for 2-homogeneous f:
  f superadditive <=> the POLARIZATION B(x,y) = (f(x+y) - f(x) - f(y))/2 >= 0

This is a POSITIVE DEFINITENESS condition on the bilinear form!

IMPORTANT CAVEAT: Weighted 2-homogeneity alone does NOT imply that f is a quadratic
form in the usual sense. The "polarization into a bilinear form" trick only works for
functions that are polynomial of degree 2. Our f(kappa_2,...,kappa_n) is a RATIONAL
function, not a polynomial, so the bilinear form equivalence does NOT hold directly.

However, there are salvageable directions:

(a) Is there a change of variables that makes 1/Phi_n quadratic? For example,
    in the n=3 case, the substitution u = -kappa_2, v = kappa_3/kappa_2 yields
    1/Phi_3 = (2/9)u - (3/2)v^2, which IS quadratic in (sqrt(u), v).

(b) Can we show 1/Phi_n is CONVEX in cumulant coordinates? Convexity of a
    2-homogeneous function f satisfying f(0)=0 does imply superadditivity:
    f(x+y) = 2*f((x+y)/2) >= 2*(f(x)/2 + f(y)/2) = f(x) + f(y).
    Wait — that uses convexity + 2-homogeneity to get superadditivity!
    Actually: f(2z) = 4f(z) by 2-homogeneity, so f(x+y) = f(2*(x+y)/2)
    = 4*f((x+y)/2) >= 4*(f(x)+f(y))/2... no, this doesn't simplify right.

    More carefully: for ORDINARY 2-homogeneous f with f(0)=0:
    f is superadditive iff the "cross term" f(x+y) - f(x) - f(y) >= 0.
    For a quadratic form f(x) = x^T M x, this cross term = 2 x^T M y = 2B(x,y).
    But our f is not quadratic.

(c) The most direct approach: just prove f(kappa + kappa') >= f(kappa) + f(kappa')
    by finding a suitable decomposition or monotonicity argument, without reducing
    to bilinear form theory.

The key observation remains: superadditivity of 1/Phi_n in cumulant coordinates,
combined with cumulant additivity, gives the main theorem.

theorem invPhi_superadditive_cumulants (n : N) (p q : R[X]) (...) :
    invPhi (boxplus n p q) - invPhi p - invPhi q >= 0

=== APPROACH 5: INDUCTION ON n ===

Can we prove the inequality by induction on n?

Base cases n=1,2,3 are done. For the inductive step:
- A degree-n polynomial p with roots {lambda_1, ..., lambda_n} can be related to
  the degree-(n-1) polynomial p/(X - lambda_n) with roots {lambda_1, ..., lambda_{n-1}}
- The convolution ⊞_n can be related to ⊞_{n-1} via a "marginal" operation
- Phi_n can be decomposed as contributions from lambda_n plus Phi_{n-1} of the rest

This decomposition would need:
  Phi_n(r_1,...,r_n) = Phi_{n-1}(r_1,...,r_{n-1}) + cross_terms(r_n, r_1,...,r_{n-1})

And the cross terms would need to behave well under convolution.
